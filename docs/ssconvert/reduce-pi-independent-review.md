The separate native profile is [numeric-native-profile.json](numeric-native-profile.json): ARM64 Gnumeric 1.12.61/GOffice 0.10.61, pinned official source SHA-256 `2ac135d856572713c1a408b76b50a59f2a9769ed21f1213446b5af255df20a12`, captured dependencies, plugins, locale, and binary hashes. ## Reproduced failures and repairs

The initial 402-case differential cohort had 381 matches and 21 mismatches. All 21 were reproduced as failing original in-memory canonical cases before implementation changes.

1. Gnumeric value_get_as_int applies fake-trunc to the reduction exponent. The one-step-near-integer inputs 7.999999999999999 and -1.9999999999999998 therefore produce #VALUE!, rather than valid truncated exponents.
2. GOffice go_reduce_pi selects reduce_pi_simple when abs(x) < 2^(27-e), with its rounded binary64 reciprocal quotient. At the rounded binary64 half-period boundary, its chosen quotient deliberately differs from mathematically exact pi reduction. The fixed-point implementation now uses this source quotient selection for the simple domain and retains high-precision remainder/full-domain reduction. The first candidate contained a factor-of-two reciprocal scaling error: the differential cohort caught 130 failures (272/402 matches), although the initial boundary regressions passed. Correcting the source scale to 2^(e-1) restored 402/402; ordinary/huge/subnormal cases now also appear in canonical coverage.
3. ACOTH(2) and ACOTH(-2) each differed by one ulp from the pinned oracle. The implementation follows source sf-trig.c reciprocal-log formulas with the odd identity for the small negative branch, avoiding the JavaScript atanh(1/x) difference at these validated inputs. Native expectations are ±0.5493061443340549.

Primary evidence: Gnumeric plugins/fn-math/functions.c gnumeric_reducepi and gnumeric_acoth; src/value.c value_get_as_int; src/sf-trig.c gnm_acoth; GOffice goffice/math/go-math.c reduce_pi_simple, reduce_pi_full, and go_reduce_pi.

## Independent high-precision verification

A separate mpmath 1.3.0 calculation at 2200 bits independently recomputed floor(pi * 2^2048); every bit of the static TypeScript constant matched. Native inputs were converted from their actual binary64 values before mathematical reduction. There were 396 reduction cases with ordinary integer exponents, including both remainders and quotient bits over every supported e=-1..7. All 126 native full-path cases matched independent high precision, including 1e100, ±1e308, the maximum finite binary64, and points at/above every simple/full threshold. Overall mathematical reference agreement was 379/396: the 17 differences are the expected native simple-path half-period decisions, not product failures. The final engine follows those native decisions.

The independent constant comparison verifies its captured bits; sampled high-precision/full-domain agreement is not an exhaustive proof over every finite binary64 value. Unmeasured inputs, rare quotient-boundary proximity, source full-path approximation differences, nonfinite direct-kernel arguments, all coercion/arity possibilities, and command/VFS effects are not passes.
