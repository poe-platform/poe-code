# `safe-bash-command-imagemagick`
`magick input.xwd output.png` decodes version-7 X11 window dumps with 8-bit palettes or 24/32-bit true-color pixels, preserving scanline padding and byte order. `magick input.ppm -units PixelsPerInch -density 36x72 output.psd` writes a Photoshop document with a composite and one editable RGB layer; `PSD:output.bin` selects the same writer explicitly. PSD output preserves alpha and 8-bit or 16-bit samples.

`-ift` and `+ift` require an input pair. Fourier support (FFTW) is unavailable; these operators warn and consume the first pair, leaving later images in order. With exactly two inputs, no images remain and output fails with status 1 without replacing the destination.

`-colorspace scRGB` uses linear RGB channels, like `-colorspace RGB`; `-separate` extracts linear components and `-colorspace sRGB` converts back. `-colorspace Adobe98` converts to Adobe RGB working channels for extraction with `-channel R`, `G`, or `B` and `-separate`; `-colorspace sRGB` converts back while preserving alpha. `-colorspace LMS` converts to long-, medium- and short-wavelength cone channels; `-channel R`, `G` and `B` with `-separate` extract them, and `-colorspace sRGB` converts back while preserving alpha. `-colorspace DisplayP3` converts sRGB into Display P3 working channels for extraction with `-channel R -separate` (or `G` / `B`); `-colorspace sRGB` converts back while preserving alpha. `-colorspace HCL` and `HCLp` convert RGB into hue, chroma and luma for channel operations; PNG output converts back to RGB. `-colorspace Oklab` converts to Oklab working channels for operations such as `-separate` and `-solarize`; `-colorspace sRGB` converts back while preserving alpha. `-colorspace YIQ` and `-colorspace YDbDr` preserve native components for channel operations and converts back to sRGB while retaining alpha. `-colorspace Jzazbz` converts to Jzazbz working channels for channel extraction; `-colorspace sRGB` converts back while preserving alpha. `-colorspace Log` applies the default Cineon transfer curve; channel separation exposes Log components, and `-colorspace sRGB` converts back while preserving alpha.

`.fits`/`.fts` and `FITS:`/`FTS:` outputs write FITS primary images, including `-write`, with 8-bit or 16-bit grayscale or RGB planes; alpha is omitted.

`magick` and `convert` execute `-list Metric` (and other lists) in argument order, preserving earlier `-write` operations, earlier errors, and later conversion output. `-list Intensity` lists the ten intensity methods without requiring an input image; list category names are case-insensitive. In conversion pipelines, `-texture image` consumes its argument without loading it or changing pixels; `+texture` clears the setting. Texture rendering is available through `montage`.

`identify -density 81x93` sets both resolution axes for subsequent inputs; `+density` restores file metadata. `magick a.png b.png -compare difference.png` replaces the image list with a difference visualization; identical images still produce output, and fewer than two inputs fail. ImageMagick (`magick`, `convert`, `mogrify`, `composite`, `montage`, `compare`, `identify`) command-line tools for `safe-bash`, powered by `@poe-code/image-ast`. GIF input selections containing only scene zero, such as `[0,0]`, read the first frame once; repeated nonzero scenes such as `[1,1]` retain both copies. Set RGB primaries and the white point with `-red-primary x,y`, `-green-primary x,y`, `-blue-primary x,y`, and `-white-point x,y`; MIFF and PNG retain these coordinates without changing pixels. `-layers CompareAny`, `CompareOverlay` and `RemoveDups` compare full 16-bit samples, preserving one-quantum color and alpha changes at zero fuzz. `-magnify` clears region and write-mask coverage so later pixel edits apply across the enlarged image. Arbitrary-angle `-rotate` resets region and write-mask coverage so later edits apply across the rotated image; right-angle rotations retain the transformed selection. `-border` expands geometry expressions for each image, including its list position and sibling dimensions (for example, `-border "%[fx:t+2]x%[fx:u[1].h]"`). `-border` and `-frame` reset write-mask coverage so later pixel edits apply across the decorated image, including with zero-sized decorations. Nonzero `-shear` also clears write-mask coverage; zero-angle shear preserves it. Use saved MPR registers as `-read-mask`, `-write-mask`, or `-clip-mask` operands. Read saved MPR registers with frame selectors (`mpr:Seq[1]`, `mpr:Seq[2-0]`) or crop geometry (`mpr:Saved[1x1+1+0]`); ranges preserve the requested order. For `info:` and `-write info:` output, `%s` and `%[scene]` preserve gaps in strictly increasing scene numbers while `%p` stays zero-based. Descending or repeated scenes are renumbered from the first scene; `-scene` explicitly renumbers the list. It validates geometry and reads the leading decimal integer: `7.5` and `7x2` start at 7, `1e1` at 1, and `0x10` or `(7)` at 0; invalid geometry such as `0xA` is rejected. `+scene` resets the setting for future inputs while preserving the first loaded image’s scene as the output numbering start. Writing `-write mpr:Seq[1]` or `+write mpr:Seq[1]` saves the whole sequence under `Seq`; the output selector does not filter the saved frames. `.otb` and `OTB:` outputs write monochrome on-the-air bitmaps with byte-padded rows and extended dimensions when needed, including `-write`. `.xwd` and `XWD:` outputs write version-7, 24-bit RGB XWD for true-color or indexed inputs, including `-write`; alpha is omitted. `.sixel` and `SIXEL:` outputs write terminal SIXEL graphics with a quantized RGB palette and binary transparency, including `-write`. `.pcx` and `PCX:` outputs write version-5 PCX with 8-bit RGB planes, run-length encoding, and density metadata, including `-write`; alpha is omitted. `.map` and `MAP:` outputs write an RGB palette followed by one-byte pixel indices, including `-write`; native readers need the image size and palette count (for example, `-size 3x1+2 -depth 8 palette.map`).

`-copy 2x1+0+0 +2+1` copies a pixel rectangle within each image. Percentage dimensions use the current image size: `-copy 50% +2+1` copies a 2×1 rectangle in a 4×2 image. Both geometry operands expand image properties, options and FX expressions, for example `-set option:src 2x1 -set option:dst +2+1 in.png -copy "%[src]" "%[dst]" out.png. A bare `-copy` width requests a square (`1` means `1x1`); `1x` copies no pixels. Fractional source and destination offsets are truncated to whole pixels before copying. Overlapping copies follow native scan order, so earlier writes can become the source of later pixels. A write mask protects the initial copy, then clears within the destination rectangle so later edits can change those pixels. `-roll`, `-chop`, `-shave`, and `-splice` resolve geometry from `%[option:name]` and `%[artifact:name]`, as well as unqualified `%[name]`. Geometric transforms (`-roll`, `-flip`, `-flop`, `-transpose`, `-transverse`, `-rotate`) require a current image: place them after the input, as in `magick in.png -roll +1+1 out.png`. Without a current image they fail without replacing the output. `-roll` preserves offsets before a trailing dimension, so `+1+1x2` rolls like `+1+1`. A comma ends the roll offsets even inside parentheses: `-roll "(+1,+1)"` shifts only horizontally, while `-roll "(+1+1)"` shifts both axes. `-transpose` and `-transverse` move write masks with the pixels, preserving protection for later edits.

`.mono` and `MONO:` outputs write headerless, byte-padded one-bit bitmap rows, including `-write` and `mono:-`. Pixels are packed least-significant-bit first with black represented by one; `-endian MSB` reverses the polarity. Farbfeld output (`.ff`, `FF:`) writes the current colorspace’s first three channels and alpha as big-endian 16-bit samples. Farbfeld sequences write numbered `.ff` files while preserving the literal destination; `FF:-` streams every complete frame in order.

`-monochrome` produces indexed Gray images (`PseudoClass`), preserving Bilevel type and image depth. ImageMagick conversion accepts positive integer `-limit thread N` caps; rendering stays serial and host resource limits remain in force. Montage preserves 16-bit `xc:` and `canvas:` color samples when writing 16-bit images. `xc:gray`, `xc:grey`, `xc:gray40`, `xc:grey40` and `xc:gray(40%)` create single-channel Gray canvases; `canvas:` accepts the same colors. Neutral hex colors such as `xc:#666666` retain sRGB channels. New `xc:` and `canvas:` images default to 16-bit depth, including raw output; use `-depth 8` for one byte per channel. `+depth` restores 16-bit depth. `null:` input creates a 1×1 pixel filled with the active `-background` color (white by default); use `-background none` for transparent black, including when reading a remap palette.

By default, `-colors` converts CMYK input to an sRGB palette before reducing colors, discarding the black ink channel. `-colors` reads the leading decimal integer: `2.9`, `2e1`, `2x3` and `2%` all select two colors. Negative counts use the maximum palette size; zero counts, including `0x2`, fail without replacing the destination. `+remap` builds a shared palette across loaded images using the current `-colors` limit and dithering settings. `pattern:NAME` renders the requested built-in ImageMagick tile, including stripes, crosshatches, bricks and gray density patterns. Use `-size 32x17 pattern:vertical out.png` to repeat it across a canvas; without `-size`, the native tile dimensions are used. Bare stdout (`pattern:horizontal -`) retains the tile format: PBM for monochrome patterns and GIF for checkerboard. With `-size`, choose an explicit output coder such as `png:-` or `gray:-`; PATTERN has no encoder. `%m` and `%f` report the source format and pattern name. Unknown names fail without replacing the output.

`-seed` preserves 64-bit integer precision, including negative seeds, and reads the leading decimal integer: `1e1` uses 1, `0x11` uses 0, and `17.5` or `17x2` uses 17. Invalid seeds fail without replacing the output. `+seed` takes no argument and resets the seed to the current Unix timestamp in seconds; a later `-seed` overrides it, including in `mogrify`. `-seed 123 -size 64x64 plasma:fractal out.png` creates reproducible plasma noise. `in.png -seed 123 -virtual-pixel Random -splice 2x2-2-2 out.png` fills exposed pixels with reproducible samples from the input. Use `plasma:red-blue` for a color gradient with plasma displacement; changing the seed changes the pixels. `in.png -seed 17 -spread 2 out.png` randomly samples nearby pixels using the chosen radius, interpolation and virtual-pixel settings; radius zero selects the native automatic radius. An attached read mask follows the same seeded displacement, so subsequent statistics use the displaced selection.

ICC `-profile file.icc` assigns a profile without changing untagged pixels; replacing an RGB matrix/TRC or grayscale profile converts pixels and embeds the destination profile. LUT-based and CMYK profile conversion remain unsupported. PNG iTXt comments preserve the actual UTF-8 text when a translated keyword is present. This intentionally differs from ImageMagick 7.1.2-31/35, which reads and rewrites the comment `hello` with translated keyword `Title` as `itle`. Language tags alone do not change the comment. PNG round trips and `-sample` preserve XMP profile bytes; `-thumbnail`, `-strip`, `+profile xmp`, and `+profile "*"` remove XMP. `-thumbnail` records the current image-list length in `Thumb::Document::Pages`, including when applied repeatedly.

Legacy PNG Latin-1 `tEXt` comments are rewritten as valid UTF-8 `iTXt`: `café` ends in bytes `c3 a9`. This intentionally differs from ImageMagick 7.1.2-31/35, which copies byte `e9` into `iTXt` despite its UTF-8 requirement. The character is preserved; ASCII comment bytes agree.

Use `-adaptive-resize GEOMETRY` for native mesh interpolation, preserving scaled virtual canvas dimensions and offsets. Write masks and rectangular regions follow the same mesh, retaining fractional coverage for later edits. Use `-interpolative-resize GEOMETRY` for bilinear resizing, or select `nearest-neighbor`, `integer`, `average`, `spline`, or `bilinear` with `-interpolate`; scaled virtual canvas dimensions and offsets are preserved. Use `-filter` to select a resize or non-right-angle rotation filter, including `Lanczos`, `Lanczos2`, `LanczosSharp` and `Lanczos2Sharp`; `+filter` clears that selection and restores automatic default filtering. `-filter Point -thumbnail WxH` applies thumbnail reduction stages before selecting pixels; ordinary `-filter Point -resize WxH` selects pixels directly. Fractional pixel bounds are rounded before aspect-preserving resizing: a 7×5 image fits `2.5x1.5` to 3×2 and `2.4x1.4` to 1×1. `-resize`, `-sample`, `-scale`, `-thumbnail`, and `-adaptive-resize` keep the original raster for scalar modifiers with signed offsets (such as `50%+1+2`, `2!+1+2`, and `2^+1+2`); `2@+1+2` fails without replacing output. Ordinary `50%` and explicit two-axis `50x50%+1+2` still resize. Area geometry (`@`) caps the ordinary resize dimensions: for a 5×4 input, `2@` produces 2×1 and `2x0@` produces 1×1; `0x2@` fails without replacing the output. `-monitor` reports resize completion percentages on stderr; `+monitor` disables them. `-resize`, `-scale`, and `-thumbnail` resample HSL, Lab, HWB, and XYZ in the active colorspace, preserving alpha when converting back to sRGB. Rotation defaults to Robidoux; Point uses bilinear interpolation by default, and right-angle rotations preserve pixels and indexed color without resampling. Arbitrary rotations promote indexed images to direct color before subsequent channel operations. Rotation and shear fill exposed pixels using the image’s stored background color unless `-background COLOR` overrides it; images without a stored background use white. `+background` resets the setting for subsequent inputs to white, preserving the background already assigned to loaded images. Nonzero shear clears an attached read mask’s selection, so masked statistics return zero until the mask is replaced or removed with `+read-mask`; zero-degree shear preserves the mask. Shear retains fractional interpolation samples from 8-bit inputs for higher-depth output and image statistics. CMYK shear resamples all four inks, keeping black separate from alpha through channel separation and conversion to sRGB. `-adaptive-resize`, `-resize`, `-scale`, `-sample` and `-thumbnail` expand image and option properties and FX expressions in their geometry, such as `-set option:dims '2x1!' -resize '%[dims]'` or `-scale '%[fx:w/2]x1!'`. For `-resize`, `-scale`, `-sample`, and `-thumbnail`, FX geometry uses the current image list: `t` is the zero-based frame index, `n` is the list length, and `u[1].w` reads the second image’s width. Later frames see earlier resized frames. Use `-define filter:blur=1.5` to widen the filter, `-define filter:lobes=2` to select its lobe count, or `-define filter:support=1.5` to override its support radius. Support takes precedence over lobes; `+define filter:blur` (or the corresponding key) clears an override. Cubic-family filters include `Catrom`, `Cubic` (also `Spline`), `CubicSpline`, `Hermite`, `Quadratic`, `Robidoux` and `RobidouxSharp`, each using its native resize weights. `Gaussian`, `Jinc`, `Lagrange`, `Sinc`, `SincFast`, `MagicKernelSharp2013` and `MagicKernelSharp2021` also use their own filter weights and support. Tune BC cubic weights with `-define filter:b=B` and `-define filter:c=C` (an omitted coefficient follows `B + 2C = 1`), or Gaussian width with `-define filter:sigma=S`. `-define filter:window=Hann` selects a windowed Sinc filter; supported windows are Box, Bartlett, Blackman, Bohman, Cosine, Hamming, Hann, Kaiser, Parzen, Welch, Sinc and SincFast. `+define filter:b`, `+define filter:c`, `+define filter:sigma` and `+define filter:window` clear individual overrides.

Identify, conversion, and montage expand quoted input filename wildcards (`*.ppm`, `?.ppm`, `[ab].ppm`) against the command filesystem in filename order, preserving names containing spaces. Unmatched patterns retain the missing-file error. Montage defaults to centered tiles in 120×120 cells, shrinking oversized images without enlarging smaller ones, with 4-pixel horizontal and 3-pixel vertical padding. Use `-geometry` to override the tile dimensions and spacing. Percentage geometry matches native sheet and thumbnail sizing: `50%` uses 50×50 cells and scales inputs twice, while `50x50%+1+2` scales once and adds padding. Positive offsets in `-tile COLSxROWS+X+Y` shift the grid and expand each sheet independently of `-geometry` padding. `-frame 2x2+1+1` sets frame dimensions and bevels, and `+frame` removes frames. Grayscale frames use the matte’s blue component for gray and preserve matte alpha, even with `PNG32:` output. `-mode Frame` adds beveled frames and soft shadows; `-mattecolor` sets the frame color, and `-mode Unframe` restores ordinary tiles. Use `-shadow` to add shadows without frames and `+shadow` to disable them. Montage supports `-label` for subsequent images and `-set label` for images already read. Labels and titles use bundled bitmap glyphs. Explicit `-font` selections return an unsupported-font error because host fonts and custom font files are unavailable.

`-shade 30x45` renders grayscale directional relief from image intensity, with azimuth and elevation in degrees. Comma and slash separators, parentheses, offsets, and geometry modifiers are accepted (`30,45`, `30/45`, `(30x45)`, `30x45+1+1`, and `30x45!` give the same relief). Colon geometry uses a ratio and unit elevation: `30:45` equals `0.6666666667x1`. Both angles are required. Angles expand option properties and FX expressions against the current image, for example `-set option:angle 30x45 -shade '%[option:angle]'` or `-shade '%[fx:w*10]x45'`.

`-enhance` reduces noise with a weighted 5×5 filter that averages similar colors while retaining sharp differences. Mean and Maximum preserve Lab and HSL working colors and alpha when converting back to sRGB. Neighborhood statistics preserve 16-bit input samples, including low-bit differences, selected channels, and Gradient subtraction. They also update selected extra channels such as `Meta0`; omitted channels, `Default`, and `+channel` include these extra channels, while `-channel R` leaves them unchanged. `-statistic Maximum 3x3` applies a local maximum filter. Maximum, Minimum, Mean and Gradient preserve out-of-range working samples for subsequent arithmetic; use `-clamp` to clip them explicitly. `-clamp` also clips selected extra channels such as `Meta0` before later arithmetic; default channels and `+channel` include extra channels. With `-virtual-pixel transparent -channel A`, Minimum and Mean include transparent border samples even on opaque RGB input, preserving RGB and interior opacity. `-statistic` updates all CMYK ink channels, including black, while keeping alpha independent. `-statistic Contrast` scales neighborhood contrast to the full color range and preserves Q16 precision in `PNG64:` output. Mean, RMS (RootMeanSquare), and StandardDeviation retain sub-byte results when exporting to PNG64, even with 8-bit input. Both statistic operands expand properties and FX expressions, such as `-set option:method Maximum -statistic '%[method]' '%[fx:3]x1'`. Window dimensions accept `x`, comma, or slash separators: `3x1`, `3,1`, and `3/1` select the same horizontal window. Colon geometry divides the first value by the second and uses height 1: `6:2` selects `3x1`, while `3:2` truncates to `1x1`. A trailing `#` preserves those dimensions (`2x1#` equals `2x1`). Put the input before `-statistic`; an empty image list fails without replacing the output. Invalid statistic geometry such as `x3`, `bad`, offsets without dimensions (`+2+1`, `-2+1`), or scalar windows immediately followed by offsets (`2+0`, `2+1+2`) fails without replacing the output; dimensions with offsets, such as `3x1+2+1` and `2x+1+2`, remain valid. Use `-morphology Dilate:2 Diamond:1` to run two dilation passes, or `Erode:2` for two erosion passes. Nonnegative integer method counts also apply to compound methods and convolution; zero leaves the image unchanged. `Smooth` applies opening followed by closing. Compound methods repeat each primitive in order (`Open:2` erodes twice, then dilates twice). `DilateIntensity`, `ErodeIntensity`, `OpenIntensity`, and `CloseIntensity` (also `DilateI`, `ErodeI`, `OpenI`, and `CloseI`) select whole pixels by Rec.709 intensity instead of taking separate channel extrema. Negative convergence counts are unsupported. Explicit flat kernels such as `3x1+0+0:1,nan,1` preserve their dimensions and origin; `nan` cells are excluded. `HitAndMiss` matches explicit kernels with `1` for foreground, `0` for background, and `nan` for ignored cells; for example, `3x3:0,0,0,0,1,0,0,0,0` detects an isolated white pixel. `Ring:inner,outer` selects pixels outside the inner radius and within the outer radius, leaving the center out. `-morphology Distance Manhattan:1,64!` computes an interior Manhattan distance map; background pixels stay black. Manhattan radius 1 supports the default Q16 step of 100, an explicit step, or `!` scaling by `65535 / (scale + 1)`. Other distance kernels are unsupported. `Thicken` adds hit-and-miss matches while preserving existing foreground; `Thinning` subtracts them using explicit `0`/`1`/`nan` kernels or the four sequential `Edges` kernels.

Use `magick -bench 2 -size 2x1 xc:red -depth 8 rgb:out.raw` to repeat a conversion a positive, finite number of times. Each iteration writes its conversion output; stderr reports the completed iteration count, throughput, and elapsed time.

## Quickstart

```ts
import { Shell, createMemoryFileSystem } from "@poe-platform/safe-bash";
import { imagemagickCommands } from "@poe-platform/safe-bash/commands/imagemagick";

const fs = createMemoryFileSystem();
const shell = new Shell({ fs }).use(imagemagickCommands());

await shell.exec("magick -size 120x80 xc:#3b82f6 -resize 50% /banner.png");
const info = await shell.exec("magick identify -format '%m %wx%h' /banner.png");
console.log(info.stdout); // PNG 60x40
```

`-print text` expands once using the first image in the current list and writes the result at that point in the pipeline, without adding a newline or changing the image list or `-format`. Both `-format` and `-print` accept literal `-list` text. A missing final `identify -format` or `-define` argument exits with status 1 and preserves earlier image output.

Read type-0 WBMP wireless bitmaps with multi-byte dimensions and byte-padded rows through buffered and storage-backed inputs. Read native OTB on-the-air bitmaps with padded bilevel rows and standard or extended dimensions through buffered and storage-backed inputs. Read native SIXEL graphics with RGB or HLS palettes, repeated columns, and six-row bands through buffered and storage-backed inputs. `.wbmp` and `WBMP:` outputs write level-zero wireless bitmaps with byte-padded monochrome rows, including `-write` and standard output. Read and write XBM C byte arrays with least-significant-bit-first pixels and byte-padded scanlines, including widths that are not multiples of eight. Decoded XBM images retain their white/black palette and 8-bit sRGB representation, including unused colors for palette cycling. XBM width, height, and bitmap identifiers use the output filename with C identifier sanitization. QOI destinations (`out.qoi` or `QOI:out.bin`) preserve 8-bit RGB and RGBA samples, including colors under fully transparent pixels. Linear RGB output carries the QOI linear-colorspace flag.

Write JSON image descriptions with `json:report.json`, `report.json`, or `json:-` for stdout. Each array record includes version `1.0`, source format, geometry, colorspace, base depth, and image properties. `-write json:report.json` saves descriptions and continues processing; `-format` does not replace JSON output. Read native ImageMagick pixel enumerations with sample ranges up to 32 bits by signature or with `txt:input.dat`, preserving HSV, HSB, HWB, YCbCr, DisplayP3, CMY and Luv color spaces, alpha and 16-bit samples. TXT input preserves header-derived depth and fractional samples for nonstandard maxima such as 7, 100, and 256. Explicitly signed TXT tuple components are skipped, shifting later channels left and filling the tail with zero. Use `-write txt:report.txt` or `+write txt:report.txt` to save a pixel enumeration and continue processing the images; `-format` does not replace the enumeration. sRGB TXT output uses the image depth by default, including 16-bit inline canvases; `-depth` overrides it. TXT output keeps LinearGray distinct from Gray, including alpha and 16-bit samples, so native ImageMagick reads it back with the correct brightness. RGB, CMYK, grayscale, and CIELab samples preserve alpha and 16-bit precision. CIELab samples retain their declared color space for conversion with `-colorspace sRGB`. Concatenated enumerations produce images in order: `magick txt:input.dat out-%d.png`; use `txt:input.dat[1]` to select the second image.

ICO and CUR inputs read images in directory order, including PNG and uncompressed DIB entries with transparency. Use `icons.ico[1]` to select the second image, `icons.ico out-%d.png` to extract all images, or `icons.ico -append strip.png` to stack them.

ICO output joins input images in directory order, preserving their dimensions and transparency. `.ico`, `.cur`, and `ICO:` write an ICO container with 32-bit DIB entries (1–512 pixels per dimension). Use `+adjoin` or a scene filename pattern to write separate icons. `-define icon:auto-resize=64,32,16` derives square icons from the first input image in the requested order (each requested size must be an integer from 16 to 512); omit the value for standard sizes from 256 down to 16, or use `+define icon:auto-resize` to clear it.

BMP output defaults to a 124-byte header and preserves the source ICC profile. `-strip` or `+profile icc` removes the profile; BMP2/BMP3 cannot embed it. Its rendering intent follows `-intent Saturation|Relative|Perceptual|Absolute`; unset and reset intents use Perceptual. Use `.dib` or `DIB:out.bin` for standalone DIB output with a 40-byte bitmap header and no BMP file header. Use `BMP2:out.bmp` or `BMP3:out.bmp` for 12-byte or 40-byte headers; `-define bmp:format=bmp2|bmp3|bmp4` also selects the version. Coder prefixes take precedence over the define. Use `-define bmp:subtype=RGB565|RGB555` for opaque 16-bit BMP output or `ARGB4444|ARGB1555` for 16-bit output with alpha. Indexed BMP output supports `-compress RLE` (8-bit BI_RLE8) and `-compress None`; BMP2 remains uncompressed.

GIF `-loop` uses a total play count: `0` repeats indefinitely and `1` plays once. Geometry forms use their leading decimal integer: `3x2` and `3/2` play three times, `12%` plays twelve times, `1e1` plays once, and `0x3` repeats indefinitely. Invalid counts fail before replacing the output. Signed counts wrap into the 16-bit repeat field, so `-loop -1` writes 65534 repeats. Use `+loop` to reset an explicit animation loop count to infinite looping without an operand. GIF rewrites retain ICC and opaque application profiles on their original frames; `-strip` removes them. Use `GIF87:out.gif` to write GIF87a without transparency, timing, loop or comment extensions. WebP output preserves every displayed frame and loop count, including positioned partial updates, surrounding canvas pixels, and `-write animation.webp`; frame delays below 10 ms become 100 ms, matching native ImageMagick. Negative WebP animation loop counts fail without replacing the output; zero loops indefinitely. WebP preserves source EXIF profiles, including camera and author tags, and updates their orientation after `-auto-orient`; inputs without an EXIF profile do not acquire one. WebP uses lossy encoding controlled by `-quality`; quality 100 defaults to lossless. `-define webp:method=0..6` controls lossy encoding effort (default 4); higher methods sample more blocks to optimize coefficient coding without changing decoded pixels. `+define webp:method` restores the default. Use `-define webp:use-sharp-yuv=true` for sharp RGB-to-YUV conversion in lossy output; alpha is preserved. False or `+define webp:use-sharp-yuv` restores the default conversion; lossless output is unaffected. Use `-define webp:target-size=1200` for an approximate lossy byte target and `-define webp:pass=6` to bound the quality search (1–10 passes, default 6 when targeting size). Dimensions and alpha are preserved; targets below the minimum encoded size can be exceeded. A zero target or `+define webp:target-size` disables the search; explicit lossless output is unchanged. Override either mode with `-define webp:lossless=true` or `false`, and remove the override with `+define webp:lossless`. Use `-define webp:alpha-quality=20` to reduce alpha precision independently of RGB quality in lossy WebP (0–100, default 100); `+define webp:alpha-quality` restores full precision. Use `-define webp:alpha-compression=0` for raw alpha storage or `1` (default) for compressed alpha; both preserve alpha precision. `+define webp:alpha-compression` restores compression. Use `-define webp:exact=true` to preserve hidden RGB in fully transparent pixels; the default discards it without changing alpha or visible colors.

Truecolor GIF output uses Riemersma dithering by default. Select `-dither FloydSteinberg` for Floyd–Steinberg, or `-dither None` / `+dither` to disable dithering, including intermediate `-write` outputs.

Use `-transparent-color color` to choose the transparent GIF palette color without changing opaque pixels or which pixels are transparent.

Lossless animated WebP inputs read every frame in order with their delays and loop count. Use `animation.webp out.png` to extract PNG frames or `animation.webp[1] out.png` to select the second frame.

Use `APNG:input.apng` to read all animation frames with their delays, blending and disposal; for example, `magick APNG:input.apng out.gif` or `magick APNG:input.apng -append strip.png`. An unprefixed PNG input still reads only its default image.

`-level`, `+level`, `-level-colors`, and `+level-colors` require a current image; put the input before the operator, including inside parentheses. On CMYK images, color levels adjust the cyan, magenta, yellow, and black planes, honoring `-channel` and keeping black separate from alpha. Numeric `-level` and `+level` arguments expand short image properties such as `%w`, as well as bracketed expressions such as `%[fx:w]`, before applying the mapping. An empty image stack returns exit status 1 and leaves the destination untouched. Numeric level endpoints accept `x` or `X` (`10x80%`, `10X80%`). A trailing `x` field does not set gamma: `10x80x2%` equals `10x80%`; use `10x80%,2` to set gamma. An omitted or empty gamma field defaults to 1, including trailing commas such as `10%,80%,` or `10%,80%,,`. `-level '25%,75%!'` applies inverse levels, equivalent to `+level '25%,75%'`; an optional gamma is preserved (`-level '25%,75%,2!'`). Gamma can also follow the white point with a sign: `-level '10x80%+2'` is equivalent to `-level '10x80%,2'`, and `10x80%-2` selects gamma -2. `-level-colors 'black,white'` expands color endpoints to the full range; `+level-colors` maps into those endpoints. Inverse levels preserve negative working samples and samples above the output range; use `-clamp` first to limit the input explicitly. Fields after the second color are ignored (`red,blue,green` uses red and blue); commas inside functional colors such as `rgb(10,20,30)` stay part of the color. Both honor `-channel`, including alpha. Unknown color endpoints warn and use black while still writing the output; `-quiet` suppresses the warning. `-auto-gamma` adjusts CMYK inks and HSL/Lab working channels before conversion back to sRGB; `-channel K` selects black ink and `-channel A` selects CMYK alpha. Default channel selection adjusts alpha too. `-auto-gamma` calculates its mean from working samples, including negative values and values above the output range. A zero selected-channel mean maps nonnegative samples to full intensity; transparent black becomes opaque white with the default channel selection. `-depth 4` quantizes existing pixels to 16 channel levels (for example, `#c8591e` becomes `#cc5522`); `-set depth 4` changes the image depth property without quantizing pixels or adding a PNG text key. PNG output coders select indexed 8-bit (`PNG8:`), 8-bit RGB/RGBA (`PNG24:`/`PNG32:`), or 16-bit RGB/RGBA (`PNG48:`/`PNG64:`). `PNG00:` retains the image depth and color mode. After `-alpha copy`, ordinary PNG preserves intensity-derived alpha, while `PNG32:` and `PNG64:` make alpha opaque; add `-alpha activate` to preserve copied alpha with those coders. `-alpha remove` preserves 16-bit compositing precision over the selected background, including for statistics and `PNG48:` output; use `PNG24:` for rounded 8-bit RGB. `-alpha associate` and `-alpha disassociate` preserve 16-bit RGB precision for `PNG48:` output and round to 8 bits for `PNG32:`. They scale the active colorspace samples, including CMYK black, while preserving the original alpha for later extraction. After `-alpha disassociate`, `-auto-gamma` and `-auto-level` discard the stored alpha plane; reactivating and extracting alpha then produces zero samples. Without an input alpha channel, `Disassociate` adds zero alpha before scaling: positive RGB samples export as white, while explicit opaque RGBA keeps its colors. `-alpha off` disables alpha without blending the colors. A following `-extent` retains those colors as opaque pixels while filling new padding with `-background`. Extracting alpha immediately afterward returns white; `-alpha deactivate` keeps the original alpha samples for extraction. After either mode, `-border` and `-extent` with opaque canvas colors keep alpha inactive, so `-channel A -separate` produces a black plane. On an RGB `xc:` or `canvas:` input, `-alpha activate` exposes transparent alpha; `-alpha on` and `-alpha set` initialize it to opaque. Explicit RGBA canvas alpha is preserved. Ordinary opaque 8-bit RGB output uses an exact indexed palette when it has at most 256 colors, with the smallest matching bit depth. For ordinary PNG output, `-define png:color-type=6` requests RGBA even when every pixel is opaque; invalid color types warn and use the default encoding. Prefixes select the encoder and are removed from the destination filename, including `-write` and stdout destinations.

Use `-encipher key.txt` to produce pixels that native ImageMagick can recover with `-decipher key.txt`. Use `magick cipher.png -decipher key.txt recovered.png` to recover native ImageMagick ciphertext with the same passphrase-file bytes. For RGB PNG output, use `-define png:color-type=2` to prevent palette conversion from changing the encrypted samples. Keep the original sample depth and channel layout when saving encrypted images.

`-swirl`, `-implode`, and `-wave` weight interpolated RGB by alpha, including transparent virtual pixels. Swirl and implode move read masks with the pixels using the selected interpolation, so later statistics follow the transformed selection. Swirl angles expand FX expressions against the current image sequence: `n` is the image count, `t` is the current index, and `u[1]` reads the second image. They support `-interpolate spline` with native cubic B-spline smoothing, retaining premultiplied RGB. They support `-interpolate blend`, selecting one, two, or four neighbors using quarter-pixel boundaries and weighting RGB by alpha. All three accept colon ratios: `3:4` produces the same pixels as `0.75`. They ignore spaces within quoted geometry, so `"2 0"` means `20`. They require a current image; placing them before the first input or after deleting all images fails without replacing the destination. `-interpolate average4` aliases `average`; `average9` and `average16` sample 3×3 and 4×4 neighborhoods for all three warps. Swirl and implode preserve pixels outside their effect ellipse. Swirl, implode, wave, and spread promote palette images to DirectClass, so a following `-cycle` quantizes the warped colors. Implode also preserves original pixels when large strengths exceed native pixel-cache coordinate limits. Both default to edge virtual pixels; `-background` alone does not change that sampling. `-wave` honors `-interpolate nearest-neighbor|integer|bilinear|average|mesh|catrom`, including alpha and 16-bit samples; `+interpolate` restores bilinear sampling. Swirl and implode honor `-interpolate mesh|nearest-neighbor|integer|average|bilinear|catrom`; `+interpolate` restores bilinear sampling. Catrom uses a 4×4 Catmull–Rom kernel and preserves native premultiplied RGB interpolation. Mesh selects triangles by luminance and divides interpolated RGB by alpha. `-wave 3/7` and `-wave 3,7` are equivalent to `-wave 3x7`; swirl and implode use the first value with either separator. It defaults omitted amplitude to 0 and omitted wavelength to 1 (`-wave 3` means `3x1`; `-wave x4` means `0x4`). It preserves native grayscale background handling, including PNG background metadata. Wave resamples external write masks with the image and preserves native RGB alpha activation for PNG32/PNG64 output. Wave and spread retain extra channels such as `Meta0`, initializing their samples to zero at the output dimensions, including zero-amplitude wave. Use `-virtual-pixel background` to retain a colored `-background` on grayscale input. `-virtual-pixel` supports horizontal/vertical tiling (`horizontal-tile`, `vertical-tile`), checker tiling (`checker-tile`), and edge-filled axis tiling (`horizontal-tile-edge`, `vertical-tile-edge`). Native names `HorizontalTile`, `VerticalTile`, `CheckerTile`, `HorizontalTileEdge`, and `VerticalTileEdge` are also accepted, case-insensitively. Axis and checker gaps use `-background`. Sequence evaluation extends smaller images using `-virtual-pixel`; the default repeats edge pixels, and `+virtual-pixel` restores that default. `-statistic` also uses the selected virtual pixels for neighborhoods crossing the image border. Gray mean statistics keep tiled border gaps neutral with colored backgrounds, preserving background alpha.

`+monochrome` preserves loaded pixels, including earlier `-monochrome` conversion. `-type Palette` and `-type PaletteAlpha` preserve linear RGB colorspace and channel values, including for subsequent channel thresholds and 16-bit output. `-colors` expands option properties before parsing the palette limit, for example `-set option:n 2 -colors '%[option:n]'`. Fully transparent source colors are grouped as transparent black before palette reduction, even when the palette has spare slots. `-remap` uses alpha-weighted color comparisons for palettes with transparency. `-random-threshold 25x75%` thresholds each selected channel independently. Omit `-seed` or use `-seed -1` for fresh randomness; other integer seeds, including `0` and `-2`, produce repeatable results. Grayscale images use one gray channel and stay monochrome, with alpha thresholded separately. Bounds accept percentages or Q16 values; a single bound uses the same minimum and maximum. Ranges accept `x`, `/`, or `,` separators and trailing offsets (`20x80%+1+2`). Omitted bounds work too: `x80%` equals `80%`, and `20x%` equals `20%`. Both `-random-threshold` and `-range-threshold` expand image properties, options and FX operands for each current image, such as `-random-threshold '%[fx:20]x80%'` and `-range-threshold '%[fx:10],30,60,90%'`. `-color-threshold '#800000-#ff2020'` creates an opaque, indexed sRGB black-and-white mask for an inclusive color range, including alpha by default. Both `-sigmoidal-contrast` and `+sigmoidal-contrast` expand image properties and FX expressions, including `%w` for the current image width and `%n` for the current image count. Read a three-dimensional CUBE lookup table with `magick in.png CUBE:look.cube -hald-clut out.png`; `CUBE:-` reads the table from stdin. The table uses `LUT_3D_SIZE` (2–256), with red varying fastest, and is expanded into a 512×512 Hald image.

Splice uses each image’s virtual-pixel method, including `Dither` for exposed samples outside the source image; `+virtual-pixel` resets the default for future inputs while preserving the method on loaded images. Use `-virtual-pixel edge` to reset loaded images explicitly. Resizing with `-sample` or `-scale` while a region is active produces black pixels, matching native ImageMagick; unchanged dimensions preserve the image. Use `+region` before resizing to sample or average the original colors. Setting `-region` replaces any active write mask; setting `-write-mask` replaces the region. Use either `+region` or `+write-mask` to clear the active restriction and restore edits across the whole image.

Use `-write-mask mask.png` to restrict subsequent pixel edits on the current images: black permits edits, white protects pixels, and gray blends the result. A smaller mask covers only its own width and height; uncovered pixels remain editable. Lookup operations `-clut` and `-hald-clut` honor the source image’s mask, including protected 16-bit samples. `-flop`, `-crop`, `-roll`, `-spread`, and `-sort-pixels` move the mask with the pixels so later edits protect the correct coordinates. `-sort-pixels` also carries an active rectangular `-region` with the pixels; `+region` clears the moved selection. `-crop 2x2@` divides each raster into a grid, honoring `-gravity` for tile overlap while preserving page offsets and output order. Offset-only crops such as `-gravity center -crop -2-1` use the virtual page dimensions after `-repage`. `-crop 16:9` fits the aspect ratio to each input and emits tiles; add offsets (`-crop 2:1+0+0`) for one crop. Percentage crops with defined gravity, such as `-gravity center -crop 50%`, select one region without requiring offsets; unset gravity (`+gravity` or `-gravity none`) keeps percentage tiling. `-crop '3x2<'` preserves the full image when both requested dimensions are smaller than the raster. An explicit bang crop such as `-crop '3x2+1+1!'` uses the requested viewport as its output page, with offsets relative to that viewport, including clipped or missed crops. `-crop` warns when a rectangle touches the right or bottom edge without containing pixels, preserving the full image and successful exit status. Fixed-size crop tiles start at the virtual canvas origin, intersect the positioned raster, and stop with a warning at an empty right or bottom edge while retaining earlier tiles. A filename crop whose width and height equal the raster keeps the full image and page regardless of offsets (`image.png[3x1+4+4]` for a 3×1 image), including palette inputs to `-remap`; explicit `-crop` still applies the offsets. Use `+write-mask` to remove the mask. The legacy `-clip-mask mask.png` and `+clip-mask` spellings are also supported. Missing mask files return an error.

Use `-interpolate nearest-neighbor -filter point -rotate ANGLE` to rotate using the nearest source pixel. `+interpolate` restores the default interpolation. Zero-degree and quarter-turn rotations preserve pixels and page geometry with `-filter point`, regardless of the configured interpolation method. Quarter-turn rotations carry the read mask with the pixels, so masked statistics keep selecting the same source samples. Arbitrary Point-filter rotations honor `-interpolate blend`, `mesh`, `catrom`, and `spline`, as well as `bilinear` and `nearest`. Rotation angles expand image properties and FX expressions, for example `-rotate '%[fx:w*10+20]'` or `-set option:turn 90 -rotate '%[turn]'`.

Shifts for `-cycle N` expand image and option properties, including `%[w]` and `%[fx:w-3]`, against each current image. `%[fx:n]` uses the full current image list length. Use `-cycle N` to rotate indexed GIF or PNG colors by a signed number of palette entries, including palettes reduced by `-colors N`. CMYK input is converted to an sRGB palette before cycling. Direct-color input is first quantized to an opaque palette of at most 256 colors, even for shift zero; 16-bit palette averages retain their precision. The first cycle applies external write masks during palette conversion, preserving the protected RGB result after `+write-mask`. `-negate` and `+negate` preserve the palette order, including unused colors and transparency, for subsequent cycling. Negation updates selected components in the working colorspace before conversion back to sRGB; in Gray and LinearGray, `R` selects the single gray channel and `G`/`B` leave it unchanged; alpha and extra channels are included by default. Select `Meta0`, `Meta1`, etc. to complement only those extra planes, preserving Q16 precision; `+negate` limits the change to pixels whose original color components are equal. As in ImageMagick, `1e1`, `1.5`, and `1x2` rotate by one entry; invalid arguments such as `0b10` fail without replacing the output file.
Use `-unique-colors` to extract a one-row palette in ImageMagick color-tree order, preserving alpha, hidden RGB, CMYK ink values and distinct 16-bit colors. Extra channels retain their channel positions and use the palette dimensions. `-alpha Disassociate -unique-colors` drops inactive alpha and orders the transformed colors without it; `Deactivate` and `Discrete` retain alpha. With an active `-region`, palette extraction preserves the source colors. This intentionally differs from ImageMagick 7.1.2-35, whose palette RGB and alpha in this case vary with uninitialized cache memory. `-alpha OffIfOpaque` deactivates alpha only when every pixel is fully opaque; otherwise it preserves all samples, including 16-bit alpha.
Use `-kmeans 2x50+0.001` to reduce an image to two cluster colors with up to 50 iterations and a distortion tolerance of 0.001. Top-level `magick -verbose` enables k-means centroid and population diagnostics on stderr; `+verbose` disables reporting for subsequent operations without changing pixel output. The operand expands option properties, for example `-set option:n 2x1 -kmeans '%[option:n]'`. Clustering respects the selected working colorspace, including HSL and Lab, and retains those results when converting back to sRGB. CMYK clustering averages all four ink channels independently of alpha and updates raw CMYK output and the palette color count. Omitted iteration and tolerance values default to 300 and 0.0001; color counts are limited to 65,536 and iterations to 1,000. Geometry accepts `x`, comma, or slash separators and colon ratios (for example, `4:2` means `2x1`). Fractional color and iteration counts are truncated; `1x` and `1x1+` use the omitted-field defaults, and a `%` suffix leaves tolerance unchanged. Zero iterations (for example, `-kmeans 1x0`) leave the pixels and image class unchanged. Use `-quantize RGB`, `Lab`, or `HSL` before `-colors N` to construct the palette in that working colorspace. `-quantize CMYK` follows ImageMagick’s RGB palette classification and CMY assignment. Use `-quantize gray -colors N` to reduce colors in grayscale; two colors produce black and white. The setting applies to subsequent `-colors` operations until `+quantize` resets it. Unknown `-quantize` colorspaces return exit status 1 without replacing the destination, even without a later `-colors` operation. Use `-treedepth N` before `-colors` to limit the color tree (depths 1–2 use two levels, depths above 8 use eight); `+treedepth` or `-treedepth 0` resets the setting.

Read indexed MAP with `magick -size 3x1+2 -depth 8 swatch.map output.png`, `MAP:input.bin`, or `MAP:-` for stdin. The size offset supplies the palette size (256 when omitted or zero). MAP accepts 8-bit and 16-bit RGB palettes, followed by pixel indices.

Read native headerless MONO bitmaps with `magick -size 11x12 input.mono -depth 8 gray:output.raw` or `mono:input.bin`; rows use LSB-first bits padded to whole bytes. Read headerless BGR, BGRA and BGRO with `bgr:input.dat`, `bgra:input.dat` or `bgro:input.dat`, `-size`, and `-depth 4`, `-depth 8` or `-depth 16`; BGRA preserves alpha and BGRO converts opacity to alpha. Packed BGR and BGRO output uses RGB channel order at depths 10 and 12; packed BGRA uses RGB order at depth 10 and BGR order at depth 12. At depth 16, BGR output retains BGR order. Read headerless RGB with `magick -size 2x1 input.rgb output.png`, `rgb:input.dat`, or `rgb:-` for stdin. Raw RGB selectors follow native decoded-window indexing: on four frames, `[1-2]` returns frame 2, `[1-3]` returns frames 2 and 3, and `[2-3]`, `[1,2]`, and `[2,1]` fail without emitting pixels. `-endian` accepts option properties before or after input, for example `-set option:bo MSB -endian '%[option:bo]'`. Read RGB plus alpha with `rgba:input.dat` or RGB plus opacity (the inverse of alpha) with `rgbo:input.dat`; both accept 4-bit, 8-bit and 16-bit samples, `-endian`, and stdin. RGB and GRAY also accept `-depth 10` and `-depth 12`: samples are packed most-significant-bit first, with each row padded to a whole byte, independently of `-endian`. Read packed cyan, magenta, yellow and black samples with `cmyk:input.dat` or `input.cmyk`; `cmyka:input.dat` and `input.cmyka` include alpha after black. Both accept `-size`, 4-, 8- or 16-bit `-depth`, `-endian`, and stdin (`cmyk:-`, `cmyka:-`). Use `-interlace Line` for channel rows or `-interlace Plane` for complete channel planes before CMYK input; CMYKA Line samples use opacity, while Plane and None use alpha. Raw `rgbo:` output writes RGB plus zero opacity; `bgro:` writes three BGR channels. Read interleaved grayscale and alpha samples with `graya:input.dat` or `graya:-`, using `-size` and `-depth 4`, `-depth 8` or `-depth 16`. Use `-interlace Plane` before the input to read a full grayscale plane followed by its alpha plane. Raw RGB and GRAY inputs also accept `-depth 1` and `-depth 2`. All raw coders accept `-depth 4 -interlace None`: samples are packed most-significant-bit first, with each row padded to a byte boundary. For RGB, GRAY, BGR, BGRA and BGRO, `-define quantum:format=floating-point` reads normalized IEEE half, single or double precision samples with `-depth 16`, `-depth 32` or `-depth 64`; `-endian` selects their byte order. Packed floating-point BGR/BGRA/BGRO reads red, green, then blue; BGRA adds alpha and BGRO adds opacity. Integer BGR inputs retain blue-first order. For 32- and 64-bit input, `quantum:minimum` and `quantum:maximum` set the sample range (default 0 to 1); `quantum:scale` overrides the range multiplier in Q16 units. Half-precision input ignores these range settings. Input defaults to 16-bit little-endian samples; RGB and GRAY (`gray:`) accept `-depth 8`, `-depth 16`, or unsigned `-depth 32 -define quantum:format=unsigned`. Use `-endian MSB` or `-endian LSB` before the input to override it; names accept mixed case, hyphens, underscores and surrounding whitespace (for example, `M-S-B`, `M_S_B` or `" MSB "`). Explicit `-endian Undefined` reads 16-bit integer samples most-significant-byte first. Use `-interlace Plane` for separate R, G and B planes or `-interlace Line` for R, G and B rows; `-interlace None` or `+interlace` restores packed RGB. BGR/BGRA Plane output also writes red, green, then blue planes (followed by alpha for BGRA); packed and Line output retain blue-first order. Radiance HDR destinations (`out.hdr`, `hdr:out.bin`, or `hdr:-`) write linear RGBE pixels without alpha. OpenEXR destinations (`out.exr`, `exr:out.bin`, or `exr:-`) write uncompressed float32 RGB or RGBA scanlines. OpenEXR scanline input supports uncompressed, RLE, ZIPS, ZIP and PIZ RGB/RGBA or grayscale channels, preserving negative and above-white samples during arithmetic and HDR output. EXR and Radiance inputs and `-fx` results retain negative and above-white samples through `-evaluate`, chained `-fx` expressions and property queries. Integer output clamps samples to its range; use `-clamp` to bound them before later arithmetic. FX retains fractional precision for property queries and 16-bit output. `-evaluate Pow` stores float32 Q16 working samples: overflow remains infinite through subsequent arithmetic, reports `inf` in `%[fx:r]`, and saturates to white in 8-bit gray output. Scalar `-evaluate` arithmetic applies to extra channels such as `Meta0` and `Meta1`, honoring explicit selections and including every extra channel by default. EXR inputs without an alpha plane expose opaque alpha to channel operations, so `-channel A -evaluate Divide 2 +channel` makes them half-transparent without `-alpha set`. Tiled, multipart, deep and other compression modes are rejected. Radiance RGBE and XYZE (`.hdr`) input supports flat pixels and planar RLE, preserves float samples, and matches native ImageMagick’s 16-bit PNG colors. XYZE channels are transformed from XYZ to sRGB for image output. Negative-X and X-first resolution lines are rejected without replacing the destination, matching native ImageMagick. Flat pixels containing `01 01 01` are read literally; truncated rasters are rejected. PFM input honors the declared byte order and bottom-to-top rows, preserving unclamped floats when converted back to PFM and 16-bit precision when converted to PNG. PFM destinations (`out.pfm`, `pfm:out.bin`, or `pfm:-`) write normalized float32 RGB or grayscale samples in bottom-to-top row order, without alpha. PFM output preserves the source byte order; inline colors default to little-endian. Use `-endian LSB` or `-endian MSB` to choose the output byte order; `-endian Undefined` writes big-endian samples. PNM destinations (`out.pnm`, `pnm:out.bin`, or `pnm:-`) select PBM for black-and-white pixels, PGM for grayscale, and PPM for color. With `-compress None`, color PNM stays binary P6, while grayscale and bilevel PNM use ASCII P2/P1. PAM input preserves alpha; use `out.pam` or `pam:out.bin` for binary P7 output with alpha. For PAM, PPM and PGM output, `-depth` from 1 to 32 sets both MAXVAL and the encoded pixel samples; binary depths above 16 use four-byte big-endian samples (the ImageMagick extension); `+depth` restores the default output depth. For PPM, PGM and PBM, `-compress None` writes ASCII samples; `-compress Zip` or `+compress` restores binary output.

Raw `-interlace Partition` reads channel files using the input stem: `.R`, `.G`, `.B` for RGB and BGR, `.G` for grayscale, and `.C`, `.M`, `.Y`, `.K` for CMYK. Alpha and opacity variants also require `.A`. Reads match ImageMagick 7.1.2 partition channel values, including zero alpha, and read only the first frame from each channel file. Packed reads with `-interlace None` retain their normal color and alpha values. Partition output supports RGB, RGBA, BGR and BGRA, writing one file per channel (`.R`, `.G`, `.B`, plus `.A` for alpha) and appending frames in input order. The named base file is neither required for partition input nor replaced on output. Raw RGB, RGBA, BGR, BGRA, CMYK, CMYKA and grayscale output supports extensions (`out.rgb`) and explicit coders (`rgba:out.bin`, `gray:-`), including intermediate `-write` outputs. Raw grayscale output converts linear and working colorspaces to Gray, including after `-alpha extract`. Joined raw output writes every frame in input order; use `+adjoin` or a scene filename pattern to write separate files. Split partition output repeats the scene suffix for each later channel, matching ImageMagick: `rgb:out-%02d.bin` with `-scene 7` writes `out-07.R`, `out-07-7.G`, and `out-07-7-7.B` for the first of multiple frames. Explicit `-depth 1`, `-depth 2`, or `-depth 4` packs raw samples most-significant-bit first, padding each row to a whole byte. `-depth 8` and `-depth 16` write byte-aligned samples; omitting depth preserves the source depth. Use `-define quantum:format=floating-point -depth 16|32|64` for normalized IEEE raw samples in the selected byte order; `+define quantum:format` restores unsigned samples. Use `-interlace Plane` for complete channel planes or `-interlace Line` for each row’s channel runs. `-interlace Partition` writes grayscale to `.G`, CMYK to `.C`, `.M`, `.Y`, `.K`, and alpha to `.A` for `graya:` and `cmyka:`, leaving the base file untouched. `-interlace None` or `+interlace` restores packed pixels; 16-bit samples retain the selected byte order. Raw RGB scene selectors such as `[1]` and `[0-1]` accept complete frames before a partial trailing frame; reading the incomplete frame still fails. At `-depth 9` through `-depth 15`, raw RGB output packs samples most-significant-bit first, independently of `-endian`, with zero padding at each row boundary for None, Line and Plane interlace. RGBA and grayscale output also pack 10- and 12-bit samples. The output prefixes `r:`, `g:`, `b:`, `a:`, `k:`, `c:`, `m:`, `y:` and `o:` write the same grayscale samples as `gray:`. As input prefixes with `-size` and `-depth`, each reads one sample per pixel into red, leaving green and blue zero and alpha opaque, matching ImageMagick; use `gray:` to replicate samples across RGB. Raw unsigned `-depth 32` and `-depth 64` output writes full-width samples: 32-bit samples honor `-endian`, while 64-bit samples use most-significant-byte-first order, matching native ImageMagick.

`-colorspace LinearGray` converts to linear Rec. 709 luminance, preserves alpha and 16-bit precision, and supports conversion back to sRGB. `-grayscale` and `-colorspace Gray` preserve 16-bit color and alpha, retaining calculated precision from 8-bit input for subsequent sepia or smush operations. Flatten, merge and mosaic blend in the first image’s working colorspace, retaining CMYK, HSL and linear RGB until an explicit conversion to sRGB. They also retain the first frame’s extra channels, such as `Meta0`, blending their samples with frame alpha; later frames cannot introduce extra channels. `-smush` and `+smush` retain the first frame’s grayscale, HSL, HSB, XYZ, HWB and CMYK color spaces and blend their channels before conversion back to sRGB, keeping grayscale output neutral and CMYK black separate from alpha. `-colorspace CMY` uses three ink channels, preserves alpha, and supports channel separation and palette extraction before conversion back to sRGB. CMYK conversion preserves fractional ink values from 8-bit RGB sources when separating channels or converting edited inks back to RGB. `-contrast-stretch` and `-linear-stretch` measure CMYK ink histograms and adjust selected inks, including black (`K`), separately from alpha. `-auto-level` stretches the selected CMYK inks (including `K`) independently of alpha; default channels share one range across inks and active alpha. `-blue-shift factor` adjusts RGB tones or CMYK ink samples while preserving black ink and unmasked transparency and discarding disassociated alpha; it requires a current image and preserves the destination when none is loaded. With `-region`, blue shift matches native alpha and protected-pixel blending. `+blue-shift operand` consumes and ignores its operand, applying the default factor of 1.5. Its factor expands image properties, option properties and FX expressions for each image, such as `-blue-shift '%[fx:3/2]'` or `-blue-shift '%[option:shift]'`. HSL and Lab edits are retained when converting back to sRGB. `-sepia-tone` retains working precision through repeated toning, level adjustments, and HSL conversion. `-sepia-tone` requires a current image and preserves the destination on failure; it keeps grayscale pixels neutral: `-channel R` or `Gray` normalizes the gray sample, while `G` and `B` leave its normalization unchanged; alpha follows its own channel selection. `-colorspace HSB` (also spelled `HSV`), `-colorspace HSL`, and `-colorspace HWB` convert pixels and report hue with saturation/brightness, saturation/lightness, or whiteness/blackness; converting back to `sRGB` restores RGB pixels.

`-set colorspace Oklab`, `Oklch`, or `LinearGray` declares how existing samples are interpreted; use `-colorspace sRGB` afterward to convert them, preserving alpha. `-colorspace LCH` and `LCHab` convert to Lab-based lightness, chroma and hue; `LCHuv` uses Luv-based chroma and hue. Channel operations use the converted values, and `-colorspace sRGB` converts back while preserving alpha. `-colorspace Oklch` converts to working lightness, chroma and hue in radians, preserving precision through channel operations such as `-solarize`; use `-colorspace sRGB` to convert back. `-colorspace Luv` converts to working CIE Luv values, preserving precision through channel expressions and `-unique-colors`; use `-colorspace sRGB` to convert back. `-colorspace Lab` converts to working CIELab values for pixel inspection and connected-component analysis; converting back to `sRGB` restores RGB pixels. Lab, XYZ, and HSL conversion promotes indexed input to direct color; a subsequent `-cycle` builds its palette from the converted sRGB pixels.

`-colorspace CAT02LMS` exposes ImageMagick-compatible working channels for `-channel R|G|B -separate`; use `-colorspace sRGB` to convert back. `-colorspace XYZ`, `-colorspace xyY`, `-colorspace YCbCr`, `-colorspace YPbPr`, `-colorspace Rec601YCbCr`, `-colorspace YCC`, `-colorspace Rec709YCbCr`, `-colorspace YUV`, and `-colorspace OHTA` convert sRGB pixels to CIE XYZ, xyY chromaticity and luminance, YCbCr, YPbPr, Rec601YCbCr, Photo YCC, Rec709 YCbCr, YUV, or OHTA, preserve alpha, and expose converted values in pixel and channel expressions. Converting back to `sRGB` restores RGB pixels. `Rec601YCbCr` preserves ImageMagick’s distinct Q16 lookup rounding and chroma offset in both directions. `-colorspace ProPhoto` converts working channels for extraction and preserves them for conversion back to sRGB.

Use `-define tiff:fill-order=lsb` for least-significant-bit-first TIFF storage, or `msb` for the default; `+define tiff:fill-order` resets it. TIFF Zip and LZW output use horizontal prediction for 8- and 16-bit integer samples. Use `-define tiff:predictor=1` to disable it or `tiff:predictor=2` to enable it; `+define tiff:predictor` restores the default. Uncompressed TIFF output has no predictor. `-separate` includes stored alpha after `-alpha Discrete` or `Disassociate`, both with `-channel All` and `-channel A`; `-alpha Off` excludes it. Reorienting with `-flip`, `-flop`, `-transpose`, `-transverse`, nonzero `-rotate`, or an applied `-auto-orient` discards disassociated alpha; a later `-alpha Activate` starts at zero. Zero-degree rotations retain it. `-crop` and `-trim` also discard disassociated alpha when cropping a raster, including whole-raster crops; `Discrete` retains its alpha samples. Separating under `-region` or `-write-mask` leaves a cleared mask on each gray plane: raw `rgba:` output has zero alpha until `+region` or `+write-mask` removes it. RGB and grayscale output retain the selected channel values.

`-colorspace CMYK` converts to working cyan, magenta, yellow, and black samples for pixel inspection, connected-component analysis, and sepia toning, preserving alpha separately. `+combine COLORSPACE` assembles planes in the requested colorspace: CMYK consumes a fourth black plane and an optional fifth alpha plane, sRGB consumes a fourth alpha plane, and HSL uses three color planes. Unknown colorspaces report a colorspace error. CMYK `-combine` fills cyan, magenta, yellow and black from operand intensities; with three operands it retains the first image’s black plane, and the fourth operand replaces black independently of alpha. With alpha enabled, a fifth operand supplies alpha and the result remains CMYK. Five operands with alpha disabled, or six or more operands, produce sRGB with alpha and extra metadata channels, using each source’s CMYK intensity. `-negate` complements the selected CMYK ink planes, including black by default; `+negate` only changes pixels whose C, M and Y samples are equal, with alpha selected independently. `-separate` honors `-write-mask` and `-region`, retaining the source red/gray sample in protected pixels. The generated planes discard that selection, so later edits such as `-negate` affect every pixel. `-channel` accepts numeric IDs `0`–`31`; IDs `10`–`31` select `Meta0`–`Meta21`, including extra planes from `-combine`. For example, `-channel 10 -separate` extracts the fifth combined plane. Missing selected planes separate as black; ID `32` is rejected. `-separate` and `-combine` require a nonempty current image list; misplaced operations fail before opening the destination. `-combine` retains inputs beyond RGBA as `meta0`, `meta1`, and subsequent channels; select them with `-channel meta0 -separate` and inspect their count with `%[channels]`. `-evaluate-sequence Mean`, `Max`, and `Min` reduce these extra channels along with the color channels, preserving 16-bit precision. `-combine` produces sRGB pixels from source intensities, including HSL, Lab and linear RGB inputs and the alpha plane; subsequent `-colorspace sRGB` preserves those pixels. Use `-channel K -separate` (or `-channel Black -separate`) to extract the black ink plane as opaque grayscale; `-channel A -separate` extracts alpha independently. Convert back with `-colorspace sRGB`. CMYK JPEG output writes four components, including progressive output. CMYK and Lab TIFF output remain unsupported and return an error before writing the requested output.

Drawing `fill-opacity` and `stroke-opacity` accept fractions or percentages, remain independent, and are saved by `push graphic-context` / `pop graphic-context`. Use `+stroke` to disable the current drawing stroke; a later `-stroke COLOR` selects a stroke again. Use `+antialias` for hard-edged drawing and `-antialias` to restore smoothing for subsequent draws. Filled circles preserve untouched 16-bit source pixels. Filled polygons and closed paths include boundary pixels and smooth adjacent diagonal pixels.

`-draw 'color x,y reset'` fills the entire image; `-draw 'alpha x,y reset'` sets every pixel’s alpha from the fill while preserving RGB. Reset ignores the seed position.

`-draw` supports `translate x,y`, `scale x,y`, `rotate degrees`, and `affine sx,rx,ry,sy,tx,ty`, composed in declaration order for subsequent primitives and floodfill seeds. Transforms reset between `-draw` options. Nested `push graphic-context` / `pop graphic-context` save and restore the transform, fill, tile fill, stroke, and stroke width. Unsupported `-draw` primitives, including the old `matte` primitive, return an error without replacing the output. Use `-alpha on -fill none -draw 'alpha 0,0 floodfill'` to remove a connected background while preserving RGB values and enclosed details. `color x,y point` replaces only the seed pixel; `alpha x,y point` changes only its alpha and preserves RGB. Use `color x,y replace` or `alpha x,y replace` to paint every pixel matching the seed, including disconnected regions; alpha replacement preserves RGB. Alpha floodfill uses four-connected neighbors and alpha-aware `-fuzz` matching (percentages or Q16 units); `color x,y floodfill` replaces all RGBA channels. Floodfill preserves 16-bit replacement colors in 16-bit output, including when the input is 8-bit. Use `color x,y filltoborder` or `alpha x,y filltoborder` to paint the connected region up to `-bordercolor`, including pixels of different colors. `-floodfill +x+y targetcolor` and `+floodfill +x+y bordercolor` require both operands; a missing operand fails without replacing the output. `+floodfill +x+y bordercolor` provides the same border-limited color painting. Floodfill seed coordinates and target colors expand image properties, options, and FX expressions for each image, for example `-floodfill '+%[fx:w-1]+0' '%[pixel:p{0,0}]'`. Floodfill color arguments are interpreted as colors even when a file has the same name. Unrecognized floodfill colors emit a warning and use black as the target color. Color floodfill uses the same opacity-weighted RGB distance and accepts percentages or Q16 fuzz units; explicit `-floodfill` target colors have zero tolerance, while `-draw` seed colors carry the image’s fuzz tolerance.

`-connected-components 4` and `-connected-components 8` label regions of equal color. Numeric connectivity values are truncated: 0 through 4 use edge neighbors; larger or negative integers also connect diagonal neighbors, matching native ImageMagick. Add `-define connected-components:verbose=true` or `-set option:connected-components:verbose true` to print each region’s bounding box, centroid, area, and mean color in the working colorspace (including hue angles and percentages for HSL), sorted by area. `-precision N` controls significant digits in component mean colors and floating-point measurements (default 6); centroids retain one decimal place. `-font path/to/font.ttf -pointsize 10 -set caption Hi -polaroid 0` renders a caption using a local TrueType font from the command filesystem. `+font` clears the selection. `label:` uses the selected font for automatic dimensions and multiline text; `-size 35x` fits the text to that width and derives its height, while `-size 35x40` preserves both axes. Font data is read only when rendering text; missing or invalid fonts fail without replacing the output. `-font path/to/font.ttf -pointsize 20 -size 140x50 label:Wing` and `caption:Wing` also rasterize the selected local font; captions wrap to the canvas width while labels retain explicit line breaks. `caption:text` wraps at word boundaries and explicit line breaks, deriving omitted canvas height from its lines (`-size 35x`) while preserving explicit height (`-size 35x64`). Local-font text uses FreeType (https://freetype.org), with a 16 MiB font limit and a 1,048,576-pixel caption limit.

`-shadow` expands image properties, options and FX geometry such as `%[fx:100-10*h]x0+2+1` for each image before resolving opacity, blur and offsets. In shadow offsets, `%n` and `%[fx:n]` use the full current image count. It preserves unsigned 32-bit PNG page dimensions when negative offsets exceed the page size, matching ImageMagick 7.1.2. For example, a 5×4 image with `-shadow 60x0-9-8` saves a `4294967292x4294967292-9-8` page while retaining its 5×4 raster. These page dimensions are metadata only; shadow rendering does not allocate that virtual canvas. Use `+repage` to reset it to the raster dimensions. With an active `-region`, the shadow is fully transparent and retains the write-mask channel, matching ImageMagick; clear the region before `-shadow` to render the usual shadow.

Read default RGB FTXT records with `magick -size 2x1 ftxt:rgb.dat out.png`. Each line contains `x,y:red,green,blue` in raster order, with Q16 channel values (0–65535). The size is required; `ftxt:-` reads stdin.

Write portable half-float maps with `magick input.png output.phm` (or `phm:out.bin` / `phm:-`); output uses big-endian binary16 RGB or grayscale samples in bottom-to-top order, without alpha. PFM and PHM output preserve the active color channels (including HSL, Lab, XYZ, YCbCr and the first three CMYK inks); use `-colorspace sRGB` before output for RGB samples. PFM and PHM output adjoin every frame in list order, including `-write` and stdout; use `+adjoin` or a scene filename pattern for separate files. PFM and PHM output include comments set with `-set comment`, writing one `#` header line per text line. Concatenated PFM and PHM inputs load every frame in order; use `input.pfm[1]` or `input.phm[1]` to select a frame. Read portable half-float maps with `magick input.phm output.png`; RGB and grayscale inputs support both byte orders and retain the original row order.

Read uncompressed, unsigned 8-bit FITS primary images with two axes and unscaled samples; FITS bottom-to-top rows are converted to raster order. Read three-plane, 8-bit RGB PCX images, including RLE compression, padded scanlines, and independent horizontal and vertical DPI. Read RGB and RGBA QOI images with lossless 8-bit samples, including colors under fully transparent pixels. QOI input reads the first complete image and ignores bytes after its valid end marker.

`-alpha discrete` makes output opaque while retaining stored opacity for subsequent `-alpha extract` or `-alpha activate`, including 16-bit samples. `%[channels]` counts the retained alpha channel after `discrete` or `deactivate`; `off` removes it from the reported channels. `-colors` and `-cycle` use retained alpha when selecting palette colors after `discrete` or `deactivate`; `-colors` also retains the reduced palette's opacity for later activation.

`-alpha Copy` reports `%[opaque]` as `True` while retaining its copied samples for `extract`; `Activate` enables their transparency, while `On` resets them to opaque. Raw `rgba:` output after `Copy` is opaque; use `Activate` to export the copied mask as alpha. `-alpha Undefined` leaves pixel values and alpha unchanged. `-alpha deactivate` preserves alpha samples for later `activate` or `extract`. `associate` and `disassociate` also preserve those samples while transforming RGB. After `disassociate`, `-alpha extract` produces white; `-channel A -separate +channel` still reads the stored alpha samples. Disabled alpha writes opaque output; `off` clears stored alpha, while `set` initializes disabled alpha to opaque. `-alpha Transparent` preserves existing alpha under black write-mask pixels; RGB-only inputs and images with alpha turned off become fully transparent. Clearing the mask with `+write-mask` preserves this result.

`-fft` and `+fft` match ImageMagick without FFTW: they warn that the delegate is unavailable and continue with the original images, including writing the destination. They do not compute a Fourier transform.
